# Weber's law, or why you're middle aged at 9

Humans experience everything relatively, and tend to make comparisons on a logarithmic scale [^a-life-time-to-brew].
For instance, if something seems only a little bit more painful, it's likely actually **a lot** more painful (think twice as many nerves firing).
This is known as [Weber-Fechner's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weber%E2%80%93Fechner_law) [^psychophysics].

$$
\begin{aligned}
\underbrace{\frac{\Delta S}{S}}_{\text{the smallest change you notice}} &= \underbrace{k}_{\text{a fixed fraction}}
\end{aligned}
$$

<small>*You only notice a fixed fraction of what you already feel.*</small>

If we consider all experiences to be felt on a log scale, then consider the span of our perceived lives.
If our life is 100% of the time we've experienced, and we'll likely live to 84, then by Weber's law, we were middle aged when we were 9 [^the-math].

$$
\begin{aligned}
\underbrace{\operatorname{weber}(\text{years})}_{\text{felt intensity}} &= \underbrace{\frac{\ln(\text{years})}{\ln(\text{life span})} \times 100\%}_{\text{life experienced}} \\
\implies \underbrace{\frac{\ln(\text{years})}{\ln(\text{life span})}}_{\text{share of life felt}} &= \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{years} = \underbrace{\sqrt{\text{life span}}}_{\sqrt{84}\;\approx\;9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Perhaps we don't truly start having experiences until 4, in which case middle age is 18 [^first-year]

$$
\text{years} = \underbrace{\sqrt{\text{first year} \cdot \text{life span}}}_{\sqrt{4 \cdot 84}\;\approx\;18}
$$

or if we consider the "unit" year to be at 23 (that was a fun year) [^unit-year]

$$
\underbrace{\text{felt years}}_{\text{how old you feel}} = \text{unit year} \cdot \ln\!\left(\frac{\text{years}}{\text{first year}}\right),
\qquad
\underbrace{\frac{\text{unit year}}{\text{years}}}_{\text{how long this year feels}}
$$

At 78% of life lived by 32, existence still seems desperately short. However, at death, with a perceived time of 102 years, you will have lived more than your years! [^youth]

```python {.marimo}
L = lifespan.value
a0 = first_year.value
a1 = unit_year.value
a = max(a0, min(age.value, L))


def felt(x):
    """Fraction of a lifespan felt by age x."""
    return min(1.0, math.log(max(x, a0) / a0) / math.log(L / a0))


def felt_years(x):
    """Age x measured in unit years."""
    return a1 * math.log(max(x, a0) / a0)
```

```python {.marimo hide_code="true"}
def _chart():
    n = 200
    rows = []
    for i in range(n + 1):
        x = L * i / n
        rows.append({"age": x, "kind": "felt", "share": felt(x), "years": felt_years(x)})
        rows.append({"age": x, "kind": "lived", "share": x / L, "years": x})
    mid = math.sqrt(a0 * L)
    kinds = alt.Scale(domain=["felt", "lived"], range=["#4e93aa", "#8a8a8a"])
    dashes = alt.Scale(domain=["felt", "lived"], range=[[1, 0], [6, 5]])

    lines = (
        alt.Chart(alt.Data(values=rows))
        .mark_line(strokeWidth=2.5)
        .encode(
            x=alt.X("age:Q", title="age", scale=alt.Scale(domain=[0, L], nice=False)),
            y=alt.Y("share:Q", title=None, axis=alt.Axis(format="%"), scale=alt.Scale(domain=[0, 1])),
            color=alt.Color("kind:N", scale=kinds, legend=alt.Legend(title=None, orient="top-left")),
            strokeDash=alt.StrokeDash("kind:N", scale=dashes, legend=None),
            tooltip=[
                alt.Tooltip("age:Q", format=".0f"),
                alt.Tooltip("kind:N"),
                alt.Tooltip("share:Q", format=".0%"),
                alt.Tooltip("years:Q", format=".0f", title="perceived years"),
            ],
        )
    )

    def mark(age_, share, label, kind, align, dx, dy, rule):
        d = alt.Chart(alt.Data(values=[{"age": age_, "share": share, "label": label, "kind": kind}]))
        layers = []
        if rule == "partial":  # baseline up to the point
            layers.append(d.mark_rule(strokeDash=[2, 3], color="#8a8a8a").encode(x="age:Q", y="share:Q", y2=alt.datum(0)))
        elif rule == "full":
            layers.append(d.mark_rule(strokeDash=[2, 3], color="#8a8a8a").encode(x="age:Q"))
        layers.append(
            d.mark_point(size=80, filled=True, opacity=1).encode(
                x="age:Q", y="share:Q", color=alt.Color("kind:N", scale=kinds, legend=None)
            )
        )
        layers.append(
            d.mark_text(align=align, dx=dx, dy=dy, color="#8a8a8a").encode(x="age:Q", y="share:Q", text="label:N")
        )
        return alt.layer(*layers)

    # Labels for the current-age markers flip to the left once the rule nears the right edge.
    side = "right" if a > 0.6 * L else "left"
    dx = -10 if side == "right" else 10
    chart = alt.layer(
        lines,
        mark(mid, 0.5, f"middle aged at {mid:.0f}", "felt", "left", 10, 12, "partial"),
        mark(a, felt(a), f"{felt(a):.0%} felt at {a:g}", "felt", side, dx, -10, "full"),
        mark(a, a / L, f"{a / L:.0%} lived", "lived", side, dx, 14, None),
    )
    return (
        chart.properties(width="container", height=320)
        .configure_view(stroke=None)
        .configure_axis(gridOpacity=0.35, domainOpacity=0)
    )


mo.vstack(
    [
        _chart(),
        mo.md(
            "<small>The dashed line is calendar time, where every year is the same. "
            "The solid line is Weber time, where every year feels a little less than the one before it. "
            f"Perceived time at {a:g}: {felt_years(a):.0f} of {felt_years(L):.0f} years.</small>"
        ),
    ]
)
```

[^psychophysics]: This comes from the field of "psychophysics", and to my untrained ear, it sounds like it belongs in an Asimov book.
[^a-life-time-to-brew]: I first wrote a variation of this post in 2019 in a love letter (love blog?). I'm not exactly romantic. It's also heavily featured in a great [blog post](https://www.maximiliankiener.com/digitalprojects/time/) and [Numberphile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hHG8io5qIU8) video.
[^the-math]: Weber's observation was that the smallest change you can notice is a fixed *fraction* of what you are already feeling:

    $$
    \frac{\Delta S}{S} = k
    $$

    Fechner added those "just-noticeable" steps up. Felt intensity $p$ grows with the logarithm of the stimulus $S$,

    $$
    p = k \ln \frac{S}{S_0}
    $$

    where $S_0$ is the faintest stimulus you register at all. If the stimulus is your age $a$, with year one as $S_0$, the fraction of a lifespan $L$ that you have felt by age $a$ is

    $$
    f(a) = \frac{\ln a}{\ln L}
    $$

    and middle aged (halfway), $f = \tfrac{1}{2}$, lands at $a = \sqrt{L}$ and $\sqrt{84} \approx 9.2$.
[^first-year]: If the first year that counts is $a_0$ rather than year one, it becomes the scaling value.

    $$
    f(a) = \frac{\ln (a / a_0)}{\ln (L / a_0)}, \qquad a_{\text{mid}} = \sqrt{a_0 L}
    $$

    With $a_0 = 4$, middle age moves out to $\sqrt{4 \cdot 84} \approx 18$. At 32, with $L = 84$, the share of life already felt goes from 78% to 68%.
[^unit-year]: $k$ is "felt time" converted back into standard years. Given a standard year at age $a_1$, set $k = a_1$:

    $$
    \tau(a) = a_1 \ln \frac{a}{a_0}, \qquad \frac{d\tau}{da} = \frac{a_1}{a}
    $$

    $\tau$ is your felt age, and each calendar year adds $a_1 / a$ of a felt year. For instance, with $a_1 = 23$, a year at 9 feels like two and a half years, while a year at 46 feels like six months.
[^youth]: From the quirk that years feel longer than your selected unit year when you're a youth.
